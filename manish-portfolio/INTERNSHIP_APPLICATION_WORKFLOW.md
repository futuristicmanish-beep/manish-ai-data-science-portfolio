### Where to Find Internships

**Job Boards:**
- LinkedIn Jobs (filter: "Internship", "Entry Level")

- Internshala
- AngelList (startup internships)
- Glassdoor
- Naukri.com (India)

- Visit careers pages directly
- Check "University Recruiting" or "Internships" sections
- Bookmark companies you're interested in

**University Resources:**
- CIET career portal
- Campus placement notifications
- College placement cell
- Career fairs and events

**Networking:**
- LinkedIn connections (alumni, professionals)
- Tech meetups and webinars
- Hackathons and coding competitions
- Professional groups and communities

**Startup Platforms:**
- Y Combinator company list
- AngelList startup jobs
- Wellfound (formerly AngelList Talent)

### Daily Discovery Routine (15-20 minutes)

**Monday-Friday Morning:**
1. Check LinkedIn Jobs (new postings)
2. Check 2-3 company career pages
3. Review university portal
4. Check 1 job board (rotate: Indeed, Internshala)

**Save Interesting Opportunities:**
- Bookmark job posting
- Save company name and role
- Note application deadline

### Opportunity Screening Criteria

**✅ GOOD FIT (Apply):**
- Role matches skills (see INTERNSHIP_ROLE_PRIORITY.md)
- Clear internship role (not ambiguous)
- Reasonable requirements for first-year student
- Company/role aligns with career goals
- Mentions training or learning opportunities
- Located in feasible location or remote

**❌ NOT A FIT (Skip):**
- Requires 2+ years experience
- Senior-level role disguised as internship
- Unclear job description
- Unpaid with no learning value
- Requires skills far beyond current level
- Red flags (poor reviews, unrealistic promises)

## STEP 2: CHECK REQUIREMENTS

### Analyze Job Posting Carefully

**Read Job Description Thoroughly:**
- [ ] Role title and level (intern vs junior vs senior)
- [ ] Required skills vs preferred skills
- [ ] Educational requirements
- [ ] Years of experience expected
- [ ] Technologies/tools mentioned
- [ ] Responsibilities listed
- [ ] Company culture indicators

**Create Requirements Checklist:**

**Example:**
```
Company: TechCorp
Role: Python Developer Intern

REQUIRED:
✅ Python programming
✅ Git/GitHub
🟡 REST API knowledge (learning)
✅ Problem-solving skills
✅ B.Tech in relevant field

PREFERRED:
❌ Flask/Django (not yet learned)
✅ Database knowledge
🟡 Machine learning basics (learning)
❌ Docker (not yet learned)

MATCH: 60% required, 30% preferred
DECISION: APPLY (good match, training available)
```

**Skill Match Calculation:**
- **80%+ match on required:** STRONG FIT — Apply immediately
- **60-80% match on required:** GOOD FIT — Apply with focus on transferable skills
- **40-60% match on required:** STRETCH — Apply only if excited and willing to learn rapidly
- **<40% match on required:** NOT YET READY — Build skills first

### Red Flags to Watch For

**Skip if you see:**
- "5+ years experience required" (for internship)
- Unpaid with no educational value
- Vague or overly ambitious job description
- Company has poor reviews with no explanation
- Role description doesn't match title
- Multiple senior responsibilities for intern role

```
Is role ML/AI-heavy? → Resume A
Is role data-heavy? → Resume B
Is role software development-heavy? → Resume C
Mixed role? → Choose based on primary responsibility
```

## STEP 5: CUSTOMIZE Application

### Tailor Resume (5-10 minutes)

**Even with variants, customize:**
1. **Update Skills Section:**
   - Reorder skills to match job description priorities
   - Add skills mentioned in JD (if you have them)
   
2. **Update Project Descriptions:**
   - Emphasize aspects relevant to this role
   - Use keywords from job description
   - Quantify when possible (e.g., "95+ Lighthouse score")

3. **Update Summary/Objective (if included):**
   - Mention the specific company and role
   - Align with company's mission or values

**Example Customization:**
```
Generic: "Built portfolio website with Next.js and React"

For Software Dev Intern at XYZ Corp:
"Built responsive portfolio website using Next.js, React, and TypeScript, 
demonstrating full-stack development and modern JavaScript frameworks 
(technologies used at XYZ Corp)"
```

### Write Cover Letter (if required)

**Structure:**
1. **Opening:** Role you're applying for, how you found it
2. **Why Company:** Genuine interest in company (research their work)
3. **Why You:** Match between your skills and their needs (2-3 key qualifications)
4. **Specific Evidence:** Mention 1-2 relevant projects
5. **Closing:** Enthusiasm, availability, contact info

**Keep it:**
- Short (250-300 words)
- Specific (use company name, role name, actual projects)
- Honest (don't exaggerate)
- Professional but personable

## STEP 8: FOLLOW UP

### When to Follow Up

**First Follow-Up:**
- **Timing:** 7-14 days after application (if no response)
- **Method:** Email to recruiter or hiring manager (if email found)
- **Alternative:** LinkedIn message to recruiter

**Second Follow-Up:**
- **Timing:** 2-3 weeks after first follow-up (if no response)
- **Method:** Brief, polite email
- **After this:** Move on, focus on new applications

### Follow-Up Email Template

**Subject:** Following Up on [Role Title] Application

```
Hi [Recruiter Name / Hiring Manager],

I hope this email finds you well. I recently applied for the [Role Title] 
position at [Company Name] on [Date] and wanted to follow up on the status 
of my application.

I'm very excited about the opportunity to contribute to [Company's specific 
work/mission]. With my experience in [relevant skill 1], [relevant skill 2], 
and my recent work on [specific project], I believe I could add value to 
your team.

I've attached my resume again for your convenience. I'd welcome the opportunity 
to discuss how my skills align with this role.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Best regards,
Manish Vidhya Narayanan
futuristic.manish@gmail.com
Portfolio: [link]
GitHub: [link]
```

**LinkedIn Message Template:**

```
Hi [Name],

I recently applied for the [Role] position at [Company] and wanted to express 
my strong interest. With my background in [relevant skill] and projects like 
[project name], I'm excited about the opportunity to contribute to [Company's work].

Would love to connect and learn more about the role!

Best,
Manish
```

### Follow-Up Best Practices

- **Keep it brief:** 3-4 sentences max
- **Be polite and professional:** No desperation or pushiness
- **Add value:** Mention new project or skill learned since applying
- **Respect their time:** Don't follow up more than twice
- **Track follow-ups:** Update tracker with dates and responses

## STEP 9: PREPARE for Interview

### If You Get a Response

**Immediate Actions (within 24 hours):**
- [ ] Reply confirming interest and availability
- [ ] Add interview to calendar
- [ ] Update application tracker with interview date
- [ ] Start interview preparation immediately

**Preparation Timeline:**

**1 Week Before Interview:**
- [ ] Research company thoroughly (30-60 minutes)
- [ ] Review job description (highlight skills)
- [ ] Identify 3-5 relevant projects/experiences
- [ ] Prepare STAR stories
- [ ] List questions to ask interviewer

**3 Days Before:**
- [ ] Review technical topics (Python, SQL, DSA based on role)
- [ ] Practice explaining portfolio projects
- [ ] Practice common behavioral questions
- [ ] Review company's recent news or blog posts
- [ ] Test video call software (if remote)

**1 Day Before:**
- [ ] Practice mock interview
- [ ] Prepare workspace (quiet, good lighting if video)
- [ ] Review notes one final time
- [ ] Get good sleep

**Day of Interview:**
- [ ] Review key points (15 minutes)
- [ ] Test technology (camera, microphone)
- [ ] Have resume, portfolio link, notebook ready
- [ ] Arrive/login 5 minutes early
- [ ] Stay calm and confident

## STEP 10: REVIEW and Iterate

### After Each Application

**Record Learnings:**
- What went well in application process?
- What could be improved?
- Was resume effective?
- Did cover letter feel natural?

### After Interview (If Applicable)

**Immediate Post-Interview:**
- [ ] Send thank-you email within 24 hours
- [ ] Update tracker with interview notes
- [ ] Record questions asked
- [ ] Note areas where you struggled
- [ ] Identify topics to study more

### Monthly Review

**First Week of Each Month:**
- [ ] Review all applications from previous month
- [ ] Calculate success rates (responses, interviews)
- [ ] Identify patterns (which roles respond more)
- [ ] Update resume based on new projects
- [ ] Adjust application strategy if needed

**Questions to Ask:**
1. Am I applying to right roles for my skill level?
2. Are my application materials effective?
3. Do I need to build more projects first?
4. Am I following up appropriately?
5. What skills should I focus on developing?

### Adjust Strategy Based on Results

**If Getting No Responses (After 10-15 Applications):**
- Resume may need improvement
- Applying to roles too advanced
- Need more projects/portfolio work
- Cover letter needs personalization
- Skills need more development

**Action:** Pause applications, improve materials, build 1-2 projects

**If Getting Interviews But No Offers:**
- Technical skills need strengthening
- Interview performance needs improvement
- Communication needs work
- Projects need better explanations

**Action:** Focus on interview preparation, practice more

**If Getting Good Response Rate:**
- Continue current strategy
- Gradually increase application volume
- Apply to slightly stretch roles

## QUALITY METRICS

### What Counts as a Quality Application?

**✅ Quality Application:**
- Researched company (15-30 minutes)
- Customized resume for role
- Personalized cover letter (if required)
- All materials proofread
- Applied to role matching skill level
- Tracked in application system
- Follow-up scheduled

**❌ Low-Quality Application:**
- Generic resume with no customization
- Applied without reading job description
- Mass-applying to everything
- No follow-up plan
- Not tracked

### Application Goals

**Weekly Goals:**
- **2-4 quality applications** (Tier 1 roles)
- **1-2 applications** (Tier 2 roles, if prepared)
- **Quality over quantity always**

**Monthly Goals:**
- **8-16 applications submitted**
- **2-4 follow-ups sent**
- **1 new project completed or improved**
- **Resume updated with new skills**

**Not Goals:**
- ❌ Applying to 50 roles per week
- ❌ Mass-applying without customization
- ❌ Applying to roles far above skill level
- ❌ Giving up after few rejections

## WHEN NOT TO APPLY

### Save Your Time and Energy

**Don't Apply If:**

1. **Far Above Your Level:**
   - Requires 3+ years experience
   - Senior or lead role
   - Advanced specialized skills you don't have

2. **Poor Fit:**
   - Role doesn't match career goals
   - Company values don't align
   - Location not feasible

3. **Red Flags:**
   - Unpaid with no learning value
   - Poor company reviews (with no resolution)
   - Vague or suspicious job description
   - "Too good to be true" promises

4. **Not Prepared:**
   - Missing 80%+ of required skills
   - No relevant projects yet
   - Haven't learned needed technologies

5. **Application Quality Would Suffer:**
   - Rushed and can't customize properly
   - Don't have time to research company
   - Too tired or distracted to focus

**Better Strategy:** Focus efforts on roles where you have genuine interest and realistic chance.

## HANDLING REJECTION

### When You Get Rejected (It's Normal)

**Expect Rejection:**
- Most applications don't result in interviews
- Even with good qualifications, timing matters
- Competition is high
- Many factors outside your control

**Healthy Response to Rejection:**
1. **Acknowledge feeling** (disappointment is okay)
2. **Don't take it personally** (not about your worth)
3. **Look for feedback** (if they offer any)
4. **Learn from it** (what could improve?)
5. **Move forward** (next application)

**Update Tracker:**
```markdown
**Status:** ❌ REJECTED
**Rejection Date:** 2026-MM-DD
**Reason (if given):** [Feedback]
**Lessons Learned:** [What to improve]
```

**After Rejection:**
- [ ] Take a short break (15-30 minutes)
- [ ] Review application (objectively assess)
- [ ] Note any patterns (keep getting rejected for same reason?)
- [ ] Continue with next application

**When to Adjust Strategy:**
- After 10+ rejections with no interviews: Review materials
- After 3+ interview rejections: Focus on interview skills
- After 20+ applications with few responses: Build more projects
