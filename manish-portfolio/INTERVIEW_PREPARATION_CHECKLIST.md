## PRE-INTERVIEW PREPARATION

### Research the Company (30-60 minutes)

**Essential Research:**
- [ ] Company mission and values
- [ ] Main products or services
- [ ] Recent news or press releases (last 3-6 months)
- [ ] Company size and funding stage (if startup)
- [ ] Technology stack used (check job posting, company blog, LinkedIn)
- [ ] Company culture indicators (Glassdoor, LinkedIn)

**Where to Research:**
- Company website (About, Blog, Careers pages)
- LinkedIn company page
- Glassdoor reviews
- TechCrunch or similar tech news sites
- Company's GitHub organization (if they have one)

**Notes Template:**
```
Company: [Name]
Products: [Main products/services]
Recent News: [Notable recent developments]
Tech Stack: [Technologies they use]
Why I'm interested: [Your genuine reason]
```

---

### Review Job Description

- [ ] Re-read the full job description
- [ ] Highlight required skills (match with your skills)
- [ ] Highlight preferred skills (note which you have/don't have)
- [ ] Identify 2-3 key responsibilities mentioned
- [ ] Prepare examples of how your projects demonstrate relevant skills

---

### Prepare Your Stories

Use **STAR Method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result):

**Story 1: Portfolio Project**
- Situation: Needed professional online presence as AI/DS student
- Task: Build portfolio to showcase projects and skills
- Action: Learned Next.js, React, TypeScript; built responsive website
- Result: Live portfolio with 95+ Lighthouse score, deployed on Vercel

**Story 2: Problem-Solving (Technical Challenge)**
- Situation: [Challenge faced while building portfolio]
- Task: [What needed to be solved]
- Action: [How you solved it]
- Result: [Outcome and what you learned]

**Story 3: Learning New Technology**
- Situation: Started with no web development experience
- Task: Learn modern web stack for portfolio
- Action: Completed tutorials, built projects, practiced consistently
- Result: Successfully deployed professional portfolio

## COMMON INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

### Behavioral Questions

**1. "Tell me about yourself."**

**Good Answer Structure (2 minutes):**
```
"I'm Manish Vidhya Narayanan, a first-year B.Tech student in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science at Coimbatore Institute of Engineering and Technology.

I'm passionate about building practical projects to strengthen my programming and development skills. Recently, I built a professional portfolio using Next.js, React, and TypeScript, which is live at manish-ai-data-science-portfolio-rho.vercel.app. This project taught me full-stack development, modern web technologies, and deployment practices.

I'm also exploring healthcare AI concepts through a learning project called ZYNETRA, where I'm understanding database design for complex healthcare systems and healthcare technology requirements.

I'm seeking an internship where I can learn from experienced professionals, contribute to real-world projects, and grow my skills in [mention relevant area for this role - AI/Data Science/Software Development]."
```

**Key Points:**
- Keep it to 2 minutes max
- Start with education and current status
- Mention 1-2 key projects with specific technologies
- End with why you're interested in internships
- Tailor the technologies mentioned to the role

---

**2. "Why do you want this internship?"**

**Answer Framework:**
- Company-specific reason (something you researched)
- Learning opportunity (specific skills you want to develop)
- Alignment with your interests (genuine connection)

**Example:**
```
"I'm really excited about this internship for three reasons. First, I've been following [Company]'s work on [specific product/technology], and I'm impressed by [specific thing they're doing]. Second, this role would allow me to develop my skills in [relevant technology from job description], which aligns perfectly with my goal to [career goal]. Finally, I'm passionate about [relevant area], and [Company]'s mission to [their mission] resonates strongly with me."
```

---

**3. "What are your strengths?"**

**Structure:** Strength + Evidence + Relevance

**Example:**
```
"One of my key strengths is learning agility. For example, when I decided to build my portfolio, I had minimal web development experience. Within a few weeks, I learned Next.js, React, and TypeScript well enough to build and deploy a professional website. This ability to quickly learn new technologies would help me ramp up quickly in this internship and adapt to your team's tech stack."
```

**Good Strengths to Mention:**
- Learning agility (with portfolio example)
- Problem-solving (with technical challenge example)
- Attention to detail (with code quality example)
- Initiative (with self-directed project example)

---

**4. "What are your weaknesses?"**

**Structure:** Real weakness + How you're addressing it

**Example:**
```
"As a first-year student, I'm still building my experience with production-level code and best practices. While I've built projects like my portfolio, I haven't worked in a professional codebase with established patterns and review processes. That's actually one reason I'm excited about this internship — I want to learn from experienced developers how to write maintainable, scalable code in a team environment."
```

**Good Weaknesses to Mention:**
- Limited professional experience (but eager to learn)
- Still developing certain technical skills (with specific learning plan)
- Less experience with specific tools (but learning them)

**Avoid:**
- "I'm a perfectionist" (cliché)
- "I work too hard" (insincere)
- Critical weaknesses for the role without mitigation

---

**5. "Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you overcame it."**

**Use STAR Method:**
```
Situation: "While building my portfolio, I encountered a challenge with [specific technical issue]."

Task: "I needed to [what needed to be accomplished]."

Action: "I approached this by [specific steps taken - researching, experimenting, seeking help, etc.]."

Result: "Ultimately, I [how you solved it], and I learned [what you learned from the experience]."
```

---

**6. "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"**

**Answer Framework (Honest and Realistic):**
```
"In 5 years, I see myself having completed my B.Tech degree and gained valuable industry experience through internships like this one. I'd like to be working as a [relevant role] where I can [relevant goal]. Ultimately, I'm passionate about [specific area like healthcare AI, data science, etc.], and I'd like to be contributing meaningfully in that space. But in the short term, I'm focused on learning as much as I can and building a strong foundation in [relevant skills]."
```

## TECHNICAL TOPICS TO REVIEW

### Python Basics

- [ ] Variables and data types (int, str, list, dict, etc.)
- [ ] Control flow (if/else, for loops, while loops)
- [ ] Functions (defining, calling, parameters, return values)
- [ ] Basic data structures (lists, tuples, dictionaries, sets)
- [ ] List comprehensions
- [ ] Common built-in functions (len, range, enumerate, zip)
- [ ] File I/O basics
- [ ] Error handling (try/except)

**Practice Questions:**
- "Write a function to reverse a string."
- "Find the sum of numbers in a list."
- "Remove duplicates from a list."

---

### Web Development Basics

- [ ] HTML: elements, attributes, semantic HTML
- [ ] CSS: selectors, box model, flexbox basics, responsive design
- [ ] JavaScript: variables, functions, arrays, objects, DOM manipulation
- [ ] React: components, props, state, hooks basics
- [ ] API concepts: REST, HTTP methods (GET, POST, etc.)
- [ ] Frontend vs Backend
- [ ] What is a framework? (React, Next.js)

**Practice Questions:**
- "What's the difference between frontend and backend?"
- "Explain what React components are."
- "How does CSS Flexbox work?"

---

## QUESTIONS TO ASK INTERVIEWERS

### About the Role

**Good Questions:**
1. "What would a typical day look like for an intern in this role?"
2. "What are the main projects or tasks an intern would work on?"
3. "What technologies would I be working with?"
4. "How much mentorship and guidance can interns expect?"
5. "What does success look like for this internship?"

---

### About Learning & Growth

**Good Questions:**
1. "What opportunities are there for learning and skill development?"
2. "Are there any training programs or resources for interns?"
3. "Would I have the chance to work on real production projects?"
4. "How do you help interns develop professionally?"

---

### About the Team

**Good Questions:**
1. "Can you tell me about the team I'd be working with?"
2. "How does your team collaborate? (In-person, remote, hybrid?)"
3. "What's the team culture like?"
4. "How are interns integrated into the team?"

---

### About the Company

**Good Questions:**
1. "What's the most exciting project the company is working on right now?"
2. "How does this role contribute to the company's larger goals?"
3. "What do you enjoy most about working here?"

---

### Avoid These Questions

❌ "What does your company do?" (Should already know)  
❌ "How much does it pay?" (Wait for them to bring it up)  
❌ "When can I take vacation?" (Wrong focus)  
❌ "How soon can I get promoted?" (Premature)

## DURING THE INTERVIEW

### Communication Tips

**DO:**
- ✅ Smile and make eye contact (or look at camera)
- ✅ Speak clearly and at moderate pace
- ✅ Use specific examples from your experience
- ✅ Ask for clarification if you don't understand a question
- ✅ Take a moment to think before answering
- ✅ Show enthusiasm and genuine interest
- ✅ Be honest about what you don't know

**DON'T:**
- ❌ Interrupt the interviewer
- ❌ Speak negatively about anyone or anything
- ❌ Lie or exaggerate your experience
- ❌ Use filler words excessively ("um," "like," "you know")
- ❌ Look at your phone
- ❌ Badmouth other companies or institutions

### If You Don't Know the Answer

**Good Responses:**
```
"I don't have direct experience with that, but I'd approach learning it by [how you'd learn]. For example, when I learned [related technology], I [method you used]."

"That's not something I've worked with yet, but it's on my learning roadmap. Could you tell me more about how you use it on your team?"

"I don't know, but that's a great question. In a real scenario, I would [research method/ask teammate/look at documentation]."
```

**Never:**
- Make up an answer
- Pretend to know something you don't
- Get defensive about not knowing

## POST-INTERVIEW ACTIONS

### Immediately After (Within 1 Hour)

- [ ] Make notes about interview
  - Who interviewed you
  - Key topics discussed
  - Questions you were asked
  - How you felt it went
  - Follow-up items mentioned
- [ ] Note any action items you committed to

### Within 24 Hours

- [ ] Send thank-you email to interviewer(s)
- [ ] Connect with interviewer on LinkedIn (if appropriate)
- [ ] Update application tracker with interview details

### Within 1 Week

- [ ] If they mentioned a timeline, wait for it
- [ ] If no timeline mentioned, follow up after 5-7 days
- [ ] Continue applying to other positions (don't put all eggs in one basket)

### Self-Reflection

**Questions to Ask Yourself:**
- What went well?
- What could I have done better?
- Were there questions I struggled with?
- What should I study more?
- How can I improve for next time?

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Practice answers that felt weak
- [ ] Study topics you weren't confident about
- [ ] Update resume/portfolio if you identified gaps
- [ ] Prepare better examples for next interview

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

### Interview Mistakes

❌ **Being Late** — Plan to arrive early  
❌ **Not Researching Company** — Always research thoroughly  
❌ **Talking Too Much** — Keep answers concise (2-3 minutes max)  
❌ **Talking Too Little** — Provide enough detail and examples  
❌ **Being Negative** — Stay positive about past experiences  
❌ **Not Asking Questions** — Always have 2-3 questions prepared  
❌ **Lying About Skills** — Always be honest  
❌ **Not Showing Enthusiasm** — Let them see you're genuinely interested  
❌ **Poor Body Language** — Sit up straight, make eye contact, smile  
❌ **Not Following Up** — Always send thank-you email
